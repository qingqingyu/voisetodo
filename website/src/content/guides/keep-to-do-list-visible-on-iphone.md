A to-do list only works if you see it. The single most effective change you can make on an iPhone: **stop keeping your list inside an app, and pin it to the two screens you already look at all day — the Lock Screen and the first Home Screen page.** Ten minutes of setup, and from then on your list appears in front of you dozens of times a day without you remembering anything.

This guide shows every placement worth using, how to set each up, and how to keep the widget content meaningful (a widget full of stale tasks gets ignored within a week — there's a fix for that too).

## Why "just open the app" doesn't work

Be honest about your own behavior: how often do you open a to-do app unprompted? Now compare that to how often you wake your phone. For almost everyone the phone wins by 30x or more, and the gap is the entire opportunity. A list that lives in an app competes for your attention against everything else on your phone — and loses. A list on the Lock Screen doesn't compete with anything; it's simply *there*, every single time you check the time or swipe away a notification.

There's a second, subtler benefit: seeing the list repeatedly does the remembering for you. "Send the report by Friday" crossing your vision six times today is worth more than any willpower technique, and it costs zero effort.

## Placement 1: the Lock Screen widget

This is the one to set up first. You look at your Lock Screen every time you pick up your phone; nothing else on iOS comes close.

**Setup (about 30 seconds):**

1. Wake the phone, then touch and hold the Lock Screen.
2. Tap **Customize**, then tap **Lock Screen** (the area above the clock).
3. Tap **Add Widgets**, find your to-do app, and pick its widget.
4. Tap the widgets to choose sizes, then done.

Not all to-do apps offer Lock Screen widgets — Apple's Reminders does, and VoiceTodo offers three styles: a rectangular one that lists your next few tasks with checkboxes, a circular one that just shows how many are left, and a single-line one above the clock showing your next task. The rectangular style is the workhorse; the count ring suits people who find lists visually noisy.

One honest limitation worth knowing: Lock Screen widgets are compact. That's not a bug — the constraint is exactly what keeps the list short enough to matter. Which leads to the last section.

## Placement 2: Home Screen widgets

The first Home Screen page is the second-best billboard you own. A medium widget (2×4) beside your dock, or a large one (2×8) if your first page has room, keeps the full day visible every time you open anything.

**Setup:** touch and hold the Home Screen → tap **Edit** → **Add Widget** → pick your to-do app → choose medium or large → place it on the first page.

Two placement tips that matter more than any setting:

- **First page, top row if possible.** A widget on page three is a widget you never see. If your first page is full, it's worth clearing space — that's the whole point.
- **Don't bury it in a folder or a Focus-filtered page.** Focus modes can filter Home Screen pages; if your work Focus hides the widget's page, the list disappears exactly during the hours you need it.

If your app's widgets are interactive (VoiceTodo's are — you tap the circle to complete a task), you'll find the widget doesn't just remind, it *works*: checking things off without an app launch removes the last bit of friction, and a list you can act on stays alive.

## Placement 3: inside the apps you open anyway

The long tail: put the list where your attention already goes.

- **Calendar:** if your to-do app can write tasks to Apple Calendar (VoiceTodo can, optionally), due items appear beside your meetings — the day's plan in one glance. This works well for time-specific tasks and less well for vague ones, so keep undated stuff out of the calendar.
- **Widgets inside Focus modes:** you can give your Personal Focus a different widget set than Work; a weekend Lock Screen that shows groceries instead of work tasks keeps each mode believable.
- **Pinned reminder:** a Reminders widget in Today View (swipe right from the first Home Screen) is the weakest spot — Today View requires a deliberate swipe — but it's better than the app's third tab.

## Keep the widget honest

The failure mode of every widget setup: the list goes stale, you stop reading it, and two weeks later you're back to "I have a to-do app somewhere." Visibility doesn't survive contact with a list you don't trust.

Three habits keep widgets worth reading:

1. **Today only.** Configure the widget to show what's due today (plus undated items), not "all tasks." A widget showing 47 things is wallpaper.
2. **Give everything a date or no date — on purpose.** Undated tasks belong in a review pile, not on today's widget. Date what's due, leave the rest for the weekly sweep.
3. **Weekly sweep.** Same time each week, ten minutes: everything unfinished gets done, pushed to a real date, broken into a next step, or dropped. This is the maintenance that keeps "today's list" short enough to live on a Lock Screen.

Do these three and the widget stays a live view of today rather than a monument to everything you haven't done.

## What not to put on the Lock Screen

A visible list is only trustworthy if everything on it deserves your attention. Two categories poison widgets fast:

- **Someday/maybe ideas.** "Learn Italian" on a Lock Screen is a daily reminder of everything you're *not* doing. Keep someday items in a separate list that only appears in the weekly review — visible tools are for now, not for eventually.
- **Other people's priorities.** Every "ask Alex about…" that isn't actually due today steals a row from what is. Date it or park it.

The rule of thumb: the Lock Screen widget should answer one question — *"what's next?"* — and every item on it should be something you could legitimately start within the day. Everything else lives further down the stack, in the app, behind a deliberate tap.

There's a pleasant side effect of this discipline: when the widget only ever shows real, current work, a glance at it becomes genuinely calming instead of mildly stressful. You're training yourself to trust the widget, and trust is what makes the reflex stick.

## How VoiceTodo does visibility

VoiceTodo treats widgets as the product, not an accessory. The rectangular [Lock Screen widget](/lock-screen-to-do-list) lists your next tasks with checkboxes — tap to complete one right from the Lock Screen — while circular and single-line styles cover the "just the count" and "just the next thing" preferences. On the Home Screen, [medium and large widgets](/iphone-to-do-widget) are interactive with the same tap-to-complete circles.

The list a widget shows is what's due today plus anything undated — deliberately mirroring the "today only" rule from above — and the weekly review exists precisely to keep that list short. Tasks can also mirror into Apple Calendar if you want them beside your appointments.

Setup is the standard 30-second widget flow, and there's no account needed to start.
