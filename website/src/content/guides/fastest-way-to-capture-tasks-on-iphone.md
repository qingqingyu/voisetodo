The fastest way to capture a task on an iPhone is to not touch the screen at all: **the Action Button (if your iPhone has one) or Siri, one press or one phrase, and the thought is out of your head.** Everything else — typing, opening apps, tapping plus icons — is measurably slower and, more importantly, demands exactly the attention you don't have when a task occurs to you.

This guide compares every method honestly, with realistic timings, and ends with the setup most people land on. A note before the stopwatch: speed isn't the whole story. A method you can use *while doing something else* is worth more than one that's two seconds faster but needs your eyes, your hands, and your focus.

## The contenders, timed

Timed on a bench, mid-conversation, tasks of typical messiness ("call the dentist tomorrow"). Your numbers will vary by habit; the ordering is what matters.

| Method | Roughly | Hands-free? | Needs screen? | Needs attention? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Action Button → voice | 3–5 s | Yes | No | Barely |
| Siri → voice to-do app | 5–8 s | Yes | No | Low |
| To-do app → hold record | 8–12 s | No | Yes | Low |
| Reminders via Siri | 5–8 s | Yes | No | Low |
| Typing in any app | 20–40 s | No | Yes | High |

### 1. Action Button → voice (fastest)

On iPhones that have one, assign the Action Button to your to-do app's "start recording" shortcut (Settings → Action Button → Shortcut, or the app's own shortcut). One press, talk, release. It works from the Lock Screen, from your pocket, from the middle of carrying groceries — which is precisely when tasks occur to you.

The press-to-record pattern also avoids Siri's occasional stumbles: no wake phrase to say, no network round-trip before it understands you, no risk that a noisy room garbles "hey Siri" into something else.

If your iPhone doesn't have an Action Button, the same shortcut is still available as a Lock Screen shortcut or in Control Center — a swipe and a tap, slower but still hands-free-adjacent.

### 2. Siri (fastest with zero setup)

"Hey Siri, add todo in [your app]" — or with Apple's Reminders, "hey Siri, remind me to call the dentist tomorrow." Works from across the room, eyes closed, phone locked. There's a reason every voice-first app ships Siri shortcuts; it's the lowest-friction capture that requires no configuration at all.

Honest downsides: Siri occasionally mishears in noisy places, there's a beat of latency before it starts listening, and in public some people (reasonably) don't want to talk to their phone. Which is why the best setup uses both — see the end of this guide.

### 3. In-app voice (the workhorse)

Unlock, open the app, press and hold the record button, talk, release. Eight to twelve seconds, but here's the thing: **the in-app flow is where the organizing happens.** This is where one run-on sentence becomes separate, dated to-dos you can confirm and fix. Capture speed and processing quality are different jobs.

A good voice-first app shows a confirmation step before saving — you glance, fix the one thing it got wrong, done. VoiceTodo, for example, turns "call the dentist tomorrow, buy milk, send the report by Friday at 3" into three to-dos with dates, and nothing is saved until you've looked at it.

### 4. Apple Reminders (the worthy default)

A word for the built-in answer, because it's genuinely good and free: Reminders' Siri capture is fast, its Lock Screen and Home Screen widgets are solid, and it's built into every Apple device you own — something single-platform to-do apps can't match. If your tasks are mostly short errands and you live in the Apple ecosystem, there is no shame in it.

Where it runs out: everything is typed or transcribed-as-is. "Call dentist about the molar, move the Friday thing to next week, and remind me 30 min before" becomes one long title, not three structured tasks. Voice-first apps spend their effort exactly there — parsing dates, splitting sentences, and putting each item on the right day.

### 5. Typing (the one to retire)

Twenty to forty seconds of high-attention work: unlock, find app, plus button, title field, keyboard, date picker, save. Typing isn't bad because it's slow — it's bad because of *when* tasks occur to you: mid-drive, mid-conversation, mid-thought. Capture that demands attention doesn't get done, and the task dies in your head. Keep typing for the minority of tasks that are easier written than said — order numbers, URLs, exact names — and let voice carry the rest.

## The setup most people land on

One tier for each situation life actually offers:

1. **Hands full / phone away → Action Button or Siri.** Assign the Action Button if you have it; otherwise learn one Siri phrase for your app. This covers the car, the kitchen, the sidewalk.
2. **Phone in hand → open app, hold record.** For the morning brain-dump and any task that deserves a look before saving. The confirmation step catches the one-in-ten misheard word before it becomes a wrong appointment.
3. **Typing as the exception, not the rule.** Exact strings and quiet places.

The whole point of tiers: the *fastest* method is whichever one fits what your hands are doing right now. A three-tier setup means there is never a moment when capturing is inconvenient — and a task that's never inconvenient to capture is a task you'll actually capture.

## Why voice memos and Notes don't count

A common objection: "I just leave myself a voice memo." Try rereading your last ten. A memo is a *recording of a thought*, not a task — it still has to be listened to again, decoded, and written down later, and "later" is where recordings go to die. The whole point of capture is to skip that second pass.

The same trap hides in note-taking apps: a note titled "stuff" with twelve lines in it feels like capture but functions as a drawer. Two weeks later you have a hundred notes and no tasks. The difference that matters isn't where the words landed — it's whether the sentence becomes a **dated, separate, checkable item** without a second round of your attention:

- Recorded → someone (you or an app) must re-process it later: fails.
- Written as one blob → must be re-found and re-split later: fails.
- Captured as separate dated items → done: passes.

That's the actual bar, and it's why "fast" alone isn't the goal. A method that captures instantly *and* organizes instantly — splits the sentence, resolves "tomorrow" to Thursday — is the only kind that removes the second pass entirely. That's the specific job voice-first to-do apps are trying to do.

## When you can't talk: the quiet fallbacks

Talking isn't always appropriate — in a meeting, a library, a shared bedroom at 1 am. Have a quiet fallback ready so the moment doesn't die:

- **Type into the same list.** Slower, but it lands in the same place, which is what matters. A keyboard-input mode in your to-do app (VoiceTodo has one) beats a quick Note that never migrates.
- **Siri with the screen down.** If you just can't speak, Siri's type-to-siri mode (Settings → Accessibility → Siri → Type to Siri) still works hands-busy — not fast, but silent.
- **The one-press Action Button still works in your pocket.** Muffled audio recognition is worse than clear audio, but a partially-heard task on the list beats a perfect memory of having *meant* to write something down.

The unifying idea: the fallback ladder exists so there is **no situation with no capture option**. Every task should have somewhere to land before the thought evaporates — whatever the room, the hour, or the social context.
