The promise of a to-do widget used to be a lie of omission: it *showed* your tasks, and then it made you open the app to do anything about them. Seeing "Send the report" twelve times a day while tapping none of it off is a special kind of friction.

That changed when iPhone widgets became **interactive** — you can act on the content right in the widget. For to-dos, the interaction that matters is one: **tap the circle, task done.** This guide covers what interactive to-do widgets can and can't do as of iOS 26, which styles are worth placing, and the honest limits Apple still applies.

## What "interactive" actually means here

Since iOS 17, apps can mark buttons inside widgets as tappable: checkboxes, toggles, play buttons. On iOS 26, for a to-do widget, that means:

- **Completing a task** with one tap on its check circle — no app launch, no unlock beyond waking the screen (Lock Screen widgets complete without unlocking at all).
- **What still doesn't work:** adding a task from the widget, editing titles, dragging items around, or any multi-step flow. Apple deliberately limits widgets to single, immediate actions — they're a remote control, not a mini app.

That constraint is the feature. A widget that only does one thing gets used constantly; a widget that tries to be an app gets neither done.

## The three placements, ranked by payoff

### 1. Lock Screen widget — see it, tap it, done

The Lock Screen is the highest-value billboard on the phone: you look at it every time you wake the phone, and on iOS 26 a rectangular to-do widget there can show your next few tasks with tappable checkboxes.

**Setup (about 30 seconds):** touch and hold the Lock Screen → **Customize** → tap the widget area below the clock → **Add Widgets** → pick your to-do app. A single-line style that shows just your next task also lives in the date row above the clock — tap the date in the customization view to set it.

The interaction detail that matters most: on the Lock Screen, tapping a task's checkbox completes it **without unlocking the phone**. Phone in one hand, grocery bags in the other, one tap and the list is current. Not every app implements this; Apple's Reminders does, and [VoiceTodo's Lock Screen widget](/lock-screen-to-do-list) does — check the app you're considering actually supports tap-to-complete on the Lock Screen, not just on the Home Screen.

### 2. Home Screen widget — the working day at a glance

A medium (2×4) or large (4×4) widget on your first Home Screen page shows the whole day. The same tap-to-complete circles apply; the larger canvas fits a few more tasks with dates.

**Setup:** touch and hold the Home Screen → **Edit** → **Add Widget** → choose medium or large → place it on the first page, top row if you can. A widget on page three is a widget you never see — placement outranks size.

### 3. StandBy — the kitchen-counter list

StandBy mode shows widgets when the iPhone is charging sideways. Same interactivity rules as everywhere else, so a to-do widget there becomes a counter-top task list you can tap off with a wet finger while cooking. It's a niche placement, but if you already use StandBy, it's a free upgrade to the same widget.

## What makes a widget die (and the fix)

Every interactive-widget setup fails the same way: the list goes stale, you stop reading it, and a month later the widget is showing a task from last quarter. Interactivity doesn't save a list you don't trust — **completing tasks is what keeps the list short enough to keep reading.** The tap-to-complete circle isn't a convenience bolted onto the widget; it's the maintenance loop.

Two habits that keep it alive:

- **One-tap honesty:** complete the task in the widget the moment you do the thing, not at the end of the day. A batch of five completions at 9pm is a chore; five single taps spread across the day are nothing.
- **The widget shows today, not everything.** If your widget tries to show your entire backlog, it becomes noise within a week. Apps that scope the widget to today's due items (plus undated) keep it meaningful — [how to set that up is its own guide](/guides/keep-to-do-list-visible-on-iphone).

## Choosing an app by its widget

If you're picking between to-do apps, judge the widget like you'd judge the app:

| Check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Tap-to-complete on **Lock Screen**, not just Home Screen | The unlock-free tap is the whole point of the placement |
| Today-scoped list in the widget | A backlog widget gets ignored within a week |
| Correct sizes (rectangular Lock Screen + medium/large Home Screen) | Single-style apps force a placement you may not want |
| State refreshes immediately after in-app changes | Stale widget = you stop trusting it |
